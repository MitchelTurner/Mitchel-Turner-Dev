On this coast

What becomes possible when the pile can be asked.

Southeast Alaska already produces the raw material. The opening is to use it before the season is a memory. These are the jobs I will take on. They are not a promise that every one of them is a model.

01

A number from a file the gear already writes.

I have trained models on sonar to estimate rockfish population health. The same kind of work fits a sounder export, a log, or any record of the water you already keep, when you want a figure you can explain.

02

The season, before it is over

Charter books, lodge nights, harbor days, and processor volume. Past years show the shape of the next one, so the totals exist before the fleet ties up.

03

The packet, read once

Council packets, grant reports, survey notes, and the binder that answers a public question. A draft or a sort. A person reads it before it leaves the building.

04

The photo and the field note

A season of pictures and logs, findable by what is in them. The crew stops scrolling for the one frame from August.

05

An answer on a bad signal

Much of this coast is off fiber. The useful tool prepares the answer ahead, or runs where the boat and the office already are.

The records stay yours

Straight answer

Most questions on this coast are not an AI problem.

Last July's bookings are a report. A phone number on a website is a page. AI earns its place when the input is a file, a photo, a stack of pages, or a question that does not have a column yet. A chatbot pasted on the homepage is rarely that job.

If a spreadsheet or a database does it for less, I will say so before any model is trained.
